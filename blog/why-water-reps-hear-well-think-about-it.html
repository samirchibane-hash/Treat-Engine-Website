Why good water reps still hear “We’ll think about it” (it’s not them)

Those four words usually aren’t about price. Here’s what’s really behind them, and how to run a kitchen table where yes is the easy answer.

SC
Samir ChibaneFounder of Treat Engine · Nov 21, 2026 · 8 min read
In this article

Your rep did everything right.

They showed up on time. Tested the water. Showed the homeowner what was in it.

They walked through the system. Answered every question. Wrote the price on the sheet and slid it across the kitchen table.

And then the homeowner said it.

“We’ll think about it.”

You know how this story ends. A follow-up call that goes to voicemail. A text that gets read and never answered. And sometimes, a few weeks later, another dealer’s truck in the driveway.

So what do most owners do?

They blame the rep.

More role-play. A new script. A tougher talk at the Monday meeting.

Sometimes that helps. Most of the time it doesn’t. Because the rep was never the whole problem. Look at what they were working with.

Count what’s on that kitchen table

Picture a normal appointment at most dealerships. Now count the tools:

  1. A test kit and a handwritten results sheet
  2. A laminated price sheet
  3. A glossy brochure from the manufacturer
  4. A calculator (or the rep’s phone) for the monthly math
  5. A lender portal on a phone, for the financing app
  6. A paper contract, or a “we’ll email it to you” e-sign
  7. A separate form for the maintenance plan

Seven tools. None of them talk to each other. And the rep has to juggle every one of them while building trust with a stranger in their kitchen.

That’s not a sales process. It’s a pile of parts.

And everyone in your business pays for it. Not just the rep.

Who pays for a fragmented sale

The rep

They spend the appointment switching between paper, a calculator and three apps, instead of looking the homeowner in the eye.

The monthly payment gets worked out on a calculator. The financing app takes forever on a phone, with the homeowner watching. And when the room goes quiet, the only lever left is the price. So they discount.

Then they drive home with no deal, no commission, and a follow-up they already know won’t get answered.

The homeowner

They just learned something unsettling about their water. Now they’re looking at a price sheet built for everyone, a brochure about a system they don’t understand, and one big number with nothing to compare it to.

They can’t see what they’re spending today. They can’t see options side by side. And there’s nothing to show their spouse except a pamphlet.

So they do the only safe thing. They wait.

The sales manager

They have no idea what actually happened at that table.

Every rep pitches a little differently. One leads with fear, one leads with price, one forgets the maintenance plan. You can’t coach what you can’t see, so coaching means riding along.

And when a rep cuts the price to save the deal, you find out after the paperwork is signed. Your margin is gone and it’s too late to fix.

The owner

You paid for that appointment. The lead, the truck, the gas, the rep’s afternoon. When it ends in “we’ll think about it,” all of that is gone.

Meanwhile you’re paying for a pile of tools that don’t connect. Your best closer is the only one who can make the mess work. And the whole business leans on that one person staying happy.

That’s not a business you can grow. That’s a business you have to babysit.

Do this math with your own numbers

No made-up stats here. Just yours. Grab a pen:

  1. What does one appointment cost you? Leads, gas, the rep’s time.
  2. How many appointments last month ended in “we’ll think about it”?
  3. How many of those did you ever close?

Multiply the first two. That’s what last month’s stalled appointments cost you. Before you count a single lost install or the maintenance plan that came with it.

For most dealers, that number stings. So let’s talk about why some reps almost never add to it.

The clue most dealers miss

Every dealership has that one rep. The closer.

Same leads. Same systems. Same price book. And somehow they close on the first visit while everyone else books a “follow-up.”

Watch them work and you’ll notice they aren’t doing anything magic. They just run a tighter process:

  1. They make the water personal: this home, this test, this family.
  2. They show the money: what hard water already costs the homeowner vs. what the system costs.
  3. They give options, not an ultimatum.
  4. They make yes easy: payment, financing and the agreement right there at the table.

They’ve basically built their own system out of the pile of parts. In their head.

That’s the real problem.

When the process lives in one person’s head, it can’t be taught, it can’t be managed, and it walks out the door when they do.

It’s not your reps. It’s your sales process.

The fix isn’t another pep talk. It’s replacing the pile of parts with one guided flow that every rep runs the same way.
